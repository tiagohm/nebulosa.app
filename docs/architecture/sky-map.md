# Sky Map

Part of the [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) of Nebulosa.

The Sky Map is the planetarium of the Sky workspace: the local sky or an equatorial chart at an instant the operator controls, with search, favorites, and the Object Inspector of the selection.
The map, its layers, and its clock live in `web/src/sky/` (`planetarium.ts`, `celestial/`), and the catalog, positions, and events come from `api/src/sky/` (`catalog.ts`, `ephemeris.ts`).
The map catalog is downloaded once per tab and turned to the instant shown in the browser; the selection's position and events are asked of the server.

## Purpose

It answers what is in the sky from the observing site at a given time, where an object is, and when it rises, transits, and sets.
It is the hub of planning: from it the operator points the mount and hands targets to the planning tools, and other features show their results on it.

## Relations

- Hosted by the Sky workspace; the [Sun Inspector](sun-inspector.md), the [Moon Inspector](moon-inspector.md), and the Satellite, Mount, and Meteor Shower Inspectors are views of its Object Inspector.
- Uses the embedded sky catalog generated by `bun make:catalog` from SIMBAD and other sources, and the positions of the [Ephemeris Chain](ephemeris-chain.md): accurate for the selection, fast for the bodies of the map.
- Draws the asteroids and comets of the [SPICE Kernel Library](spice-kernel-library.md) and follows its generation; looks minor bodies up in JPL SBDB and Horizons.
- Observes from the active site and shows the profile's display zone (see [Observing Sites and Time Zones](observing-sites-and-time-zones.md)).
- Shows and follows the mount of the active profile; Slew to Object and Slew to Point run as a `mount.slew` operation that holds the mount until the slew ends (see [Device Resource Matrix](device-resource-matrix.md)).
- Draws the satellites of [Artificial Satellites](artificial-satellites.md), the radiants of [Meteor Showers](meteor-showers.md), and the framings of [Framing](framing.md) around the selection.
- Receives coordinates and footprints from solved images (see [Plate Solving](plate-solving.md)) and targets from the [Observing Planner](observing-planner.md) and the [Jupiter](jupiter-observing-planner.md) and [Saturn](saturn-observing-planner.md) Observing Planners.
- Opens [Framing](framing.md), the [Observing Planner](observing-planner.md), and the [Orbital Mechanics Explorer](orbital-mechanics-explorer.md) on the selection.
- Keeps its favorites and display preferences in the profile (see [Persistence and State](persistence-and-state.md)); satellite favorites are those of Artificial Satellites.

## Rules and Limitations

- The projection is fixed: centering on a coordinate only pans the view, and a point the map cannot project, or a selection below the horizon, is said so rather than ignored.
- Positions handed to or from the map are J2000; the map converts them to the frame of its data, so they line up with the stars drawn.
- An empty click selects at most one Sky Point, a fixed coordinate that can be slewed to, framed, or planned, but never a favorite.
- A meteor radiant is a direction, not an object: it is never slewed to, framed, or added to a plan.
- Handoffs to and from the map never move the Sky clock, the mount, the profile, or its site, except the Show in Sky of the planetary planners, which sets the Sky clock.
- Every request of the process to JPL (Horizons, SBDB, CAD) goes through one shared queue (`api/src/ssd.ts`), one at a time as its fair-use policy asks; a refusal pauses the queue instead of being retried, so an accurate position may wait.
- Offline, Horizons and SBDB are never asked, and a minor body without kernels or cached elements has no position.
- Catalog stars stop at magnitude 8.
