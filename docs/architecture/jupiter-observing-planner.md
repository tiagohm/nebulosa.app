# Jupiter Observing Planner Contract

Part of the [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) of Nebulosa.

- The Jupiter Observing Planner (`api/src/jupiter.ts` with `jupiter.astronomy.ts` and `jupiter.store.ts`) predicts, on request and read-only, the view of Jupiter and its Galilean satellites at an instant (`POST /state`), their sampled tracks over a range (`POST /track`), and the phenomena over a range (`POST /events`): Great Red Spot transits, satellite transits, shadow transits, satellite occultations and eclipses, and mutual occultations and eclipses. It moves no device, changes no Sky clock, site, or profile, starts no operation, registers nothing in the operation registry, schedules nothing, and never asks a remote service; only the Great Red Spot model is stored.
- Source: every computation takes all its states (the Sun, the Earth, Jupiter's centre 599, never the barycenter 5, and 501–504) from one source chosen once per request: the SPICE kernels when the set has every Jovian body, through `spkJovianEphemeris` of the toolkit, else the analytical VSOP87E + L1.2 theories. A `JovianEphemerisMiss` anywhere in a kernel computation discards it and reruns it whole from the theories under `automatic` and `offline` (`fallbackReason` and the missed `target`, NAIF `body`, and `time` in the source), or answers `503` under `spkOnly` (`coverageUnavailable` for an epoch out of coverage, `unsupportedKernel` for an unevaluable selected segment, `missingDependency` otherwise, with the `reason` and `miss`). Horizons is never asked, whatever the policy. The fallback reasons are `noKernel`, `loading`, `targetMissing`, `missingCenter`, `outOfCoverage`, `unsupportedFrame`, `unsupportedType`, and `cycle`; a disabled kernel is outside the set, so it shows as `targetMissing`. Dense scans interpolate the source on a fixed grid (`interpolatedJovianEphemeris`), whose nodes reach up to three days beyond the scan for the planets, so a scan within three days of the end of a kernel's coverage falls back. The orchestration (source choice, chunks, restarts, readiness probes, and the 503 codes) is the planner-agnostic `withSatelliteSource` of `api/src/planetary.source.ts`, shared with the Saturn planner.
- Kernel lifetime: scans run in chunks (a day of events, 250 track samples) that yield to the event loop; after each yield the kernel snapshot is read again and a kernel computation restarts from its first chunk when the generation changed, at most twice, then answers `503 generationChanged`. A kernel set is never read across a yield of another generation, so a rebuild that closes its files is safe. An aborted request stops between chunks and is not answered; nothing partial is returned.
- Signature (`JupiterSignature`) of every answer: the model version (`JUPITER_MODEL_VERSION`, 1), the source (`spk` with the kernel ids that supplied each body, or `analytical` with its fallback reason), the policy, the kernel generation, and the Great Red Spot revision when the model was used. Event and track answers are kept in `LruCache` entries (16 each) keyed by every input of the request, the model version, the policy, the generation, and the Great Red Spot revision; nothing derived is persisted.
- Geometry and contacts (see the toolkit's Jovian Satellite Geometry and Galilean Satellite Phenomena): geocentric observed instants, never topocentric; an oblate Jupiter (IAU 1-bar radii) and spherical moons; finite-Sun shadow cones. Each event carries only the contacts actually reached (`external1`, `internal1`, `center1`, `center2`, `internal2`, `external2`, and for a shadow transit `visibleStart`/`visibleEnd` while its centre faces the Earth); an eclipse's external contacts are the penumbra and its centre and internal contacts the umbra; a mutual event has only its first and last contacts and its `impactParameter`, with no photometry. `start` and `end` are the external contacts (the maximum when one is missing), `startsBeforeRange` and `endsAfterRange` flag an event reaching beyond the range, `overlaps` lists the ids of the events whose intervals intersect, and the `id` is the kind, the moons, and the maximum to the second. The scan covers the range padded by six hours in day chunks whose events are kept by their maximum, so no event is reported twice.
- Great Red Spot: one server-global model (`jupiter_grs_settings`, `JupiterGrsStore`): the System II west longitude (radians) at a reference instant, a drift in radians per day (positive toward increasing west longitude), and optional source label and notes, entered by the operator and never fetched or defaulted. `PUT /grs` saves it from a revision (`409 conflict` with the current model under `grs`, `500 writeFailed`) and publishes `jupiter:grsUpdated`. Without a model no transit is predicted and everything else works; a reference more than 60 days from the instant asked about is `stale` but still used. A transit is the System II central meridian crossing the drifting longitude on the near side, with a viewing window while the spot is within 30° of the central meridian, labeled as a window, not contacts. The marker of `/state` is drawn at a schematic 22° S, a display assumption.
- Visibility (`POST /events` with a `site`): the nights of the site over the padded range come from the Astro Weather nights (`AstroWeatherService.night`, kept 20 per site by the planner's `SiteNightCache`; the nights, windows, visibility, overlaps, and ranking, and the physically visible part of a moon event with the intervals each moon is hidden, are the shared `api/src/planetary.visibility.ts`, which the Saturn planner uses too) as `PlanningNight`s of the Observing Planner, and Jupiter's watchable windows are those of the planetary preset (Jupiter's geometric altitude at least 25°, the Sun at most −6°) from its local VSOP87E position, which only decides when a phenomenon can be watched. An event's watchable part is its interval inside those windows and physically visible: a shadow transit while its centre faces the Earth, an eclipse while the moon is not behind the disk, a mutual event while neither moon is behind the disk or in the umbra. The status is `observable`, `partial`, `notObservable`, or `unknownNoSite` without a site; the reasons are `sunTooHigh`, `jupiterTooLow`, `moonHidden`, and `shadowHidden`; the evening date is that of the night holding the maximum; the rank orders the watchable events by their longest watchable part, then by Jupiter's peak altitude, without hiding any event.
- Tracks (`POST /track`): one sample per whole number of minutes, at most 2000 per moon, with each moon's sky offsets east and north on the true equator of the middle of the range and its depth, in Jupiter equatorial radii, and its disk and eclipse states; for display and interpolation only, never for contacts. `POST /source` probes every Jovian body every six hours of a range (`readyForJupiter`, `partialCoverage`, `unsupported`, `missingDependencies`) to explain what the kernels lack; it is not a guarantee for a scan, and no readiness is stored.
- Limits: the routes trust their requests (the tool asks for ranges of at most 14 days); the first visibility of a site computes each of its nights synchronously through the shared Astro Weather night (a few hundred milliseconds each, including its Moon), later ones come from the cache.
