# Calculator Contract

Part of the [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) of Nebulosa.

The Calculator tool of the Reference group: task-oriented calculators that run entirely in the browser.

- Calculator (`web/src/tools/calculator/`, `/tools/calculator[/<calculator id>]`): task-oriented calculators chosen in one menu grouped by category (`catalog.ts`), each composing pure formulas of `nebulosa/src/astronomy/formulas` behind one form, never one entry per formula and no expression engine; the URL names the calculator shown (the first one when it names none or an unknown one), so a reload or a link reopens it.
  The five categories are Optics & Field of View (Imaging Setup: field, diagonal, image scale, effective focal length and ratio through `resolveFraming`, so it matches the 2·atan convention of the framings, with no image scale for a sensor given by its physical size, its sensor filled from a model of the camera model catalog (`imagingSetupFromModel`), leaving the optics as they are, and its native focal length and aperture from a model of the telescope model catalog (`imagingSetupFromTelescopeModel`), leaving the reducer and the sensor as they are; Mosaic Estimator: rectangular panel count per axis with overlap, not an optimized layout), Sampling & Focus (Sampling: FWHM in pixels from the seeing or the seeing from a measured FWHM, the 2–3 px rule-of-thumb class, and the focal length for a target FWHM; Diffraction & Focus: Airy disk and critical focus zone, wavelength in nanometers; both take the pixel size from a camera model when chosen, and Diffraction & Focus the native focal ratio from a telescope model that gives its focal length and aperture), Exposure & Integration (Exposure Planner: total integration from a frame count or whole frames for a target time, with the idealized √N and 1.25·log₁₀N stacking gains; Star Trailing: sidereal drift of an untracked star and the longest exposure within a trail budget, none at a celestial pole), Visual Observation (Eyepiece & Telescope, whose native focal length and aperture a telescope model fills: magnification, exit pupil, true field from the apparent field or the field stop, Dawes and Rayleigh limits, limiting magnitude; Aperture Comparison, each aperture from a telescope model: clear light grasp and the collecting area left by central obstructions, never presented as resolution), and Atmosphere & Conditions (Airmass & Visibility Effects: Kasten–Young airmass, extinction for a coefficient, Sæmundsson refraction; Dew Conditions: Magnus dew point with the heuristic ambient-margin risk, humidity from a dew point, or frost point from a humidity relative to ice).
  Each calculator shows its formula, units, domain, and approximations; mode switches fix the direction of a calculation so fields never depend on each other circularly.
  Camera & Signal and Guiding & Tracking calculators are not provided yet.
  Everything runs in the browser: no route, event, operation, or device is involved.
  Fields keep their text as typed and are parsed only at the calculation boundary (`values.ts`), so an empty or out-of-domain field shows what to enter or why it cannot be used instead of a previous result, and no result shows NaN or Infinity.
  A field the calculation cannot use is drawn in the danger colour with the reason under it (`FieldIssue`), the reason naming the unit of a bounded domain; focal lengths, apertures, reducer and Barlow factors, and pixel sizes are bounded to real optics, so a typo never gives a field or scale that cannot be written, and an Imaging Setup aperture that cannot be used only loses the focal ratio.
  A field has the frame of `NumberInput` (`StepperFrame`) without its number model: its − and + buttons, like Arrow Up and Down, step the last typed decimal (by ten with Shift on the keys) and are disabled while the field holds no number, keystrokes that cannot be part of a number are refused, and the text is never rounded, snapped, or clamped; a field that may be negative (a declination, a temperature, a dew point) opens the full keyboard on phones, whose decimal keypad has no minus sign.
  Fields sit two per row from `sm`, one per row from `md`, where the inputs share the tool with the results, and two again from `lg`, so the step buttons always leave room for the number.
  The principal result is the tool's readout, a native `<output>`; below `md`, where the results follow the inputs, a one-line summary of it, or of what is missing or wrong, stays pinned at the bottom of the tool.
  Each calculator keeps a draft in React state while the tool is open, never persisted, and a change of active profile starts over.
  A calculator that imports optics shows the Main Camera of the Equipment Setup until its draft is first edited, imported into, or reset (`shownEntry`), when the setup fills every field it imports, and says so; otherwise, and for the calculators that import nothing, the draft starts from a labeled example, and a calculator that imports nothing says so in one line with its reset instead of a card.
  An optical configuration can be imported by value (`sources.ts`, `importDraft`) from the Main or Guide Camera (the telescope or guide telescope with the sensor of that camera role, what its device reports completed by the Equipment Catalog as the framings read it (`roleSensors`), never another camera's, refused while the role has none), from a camera of the Equipment Catalog that is not archived (the telescope with the sensor it declares, refused when it declares no sensor), from a telescope of the Equipment Catalog that is not archived (its native focal length and aperture, without reducer or Barlow, with the sensor of the Main Camera, refused when it declares no focal length), or from a framing of the Framing tool, as the tab resolves it;
  the menu groups the sources under Equipment Setup, Catalog cameras, Catalog telescopes, and Framings, and the status names what an import of the catalog combined (a camera behind the telescope of the Equipment Setup, a telescope with the Main Camera);
  an import never empties a field: each value the source does not give (an optic or a pixel size not entered or declared) keeps the field as it was, and the status says so;
  the telescope and guide telescope of the Equipment Setup are read resolved through the telescope of the Equipment Catalog each refers to;
  the draft is then edited on its own, live changes never rewrite it, its origin is shown only while the same camera is followed, and the profile, presets, and devices are never written.
