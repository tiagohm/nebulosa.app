# Image Stacking Contract

Part of the [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) of Nebulosa.

Image Stacking turns light frames saved on the server into per-filter integrations and color composites. It needs no connection, claims no device, and takes no part in `OperationRegistry`. It covers the project model (importing, grouping, and reviewing frames), the planning of a stack (calibration decisions, the measurement and registration of every frame, and validation), its integration into one linear master per filter, and the color composition of those masters. Drizzle is not provided, and masters and composites carry no WCS.

- **Projects.** A project (`stack_projects`) is one intended target or field: a name an operator gives it (`POST /projects`) and edits from its revision (`PATCH /projects/:projectId`, `409 conflict` otherwise), and the light frames chosen for it. Deleting a project (`DELETE /projects/:projectId`) deletes its rows, composites, and products, never a frame file.
- **Imports.** `POST /projects/:projectId/imports` with `{ paths, recursive }` starts a cancellable scan of headers of directories and single files (`scanDirectory` and `inspectFile` of the Calibration Library, bounded depth, count, and concurrency), never pixels; nothing is written, moved, or copied. A path inside the directories of Nebulosa (`image-stacking/` and `calibration-library/` under the data directory) is refused (`400 managedPath`), and a candidate inside one cannot be added. A file reached through two names, in one scan or across imports, is one frame (its device:inode identity, `UNIQUE (project_id, identity)`), and a known one is not added twice. A completed import reports how many lights and unclassified files a commit would add, and its review lists the candidates by page with their role, filter, target, and exposure; `/imports/:jobId/commit` adds lights, and files whose header says no role either declared lights or kept unclassified: every usable candidate, only those it names (`paths`), or all but those it skips (`skipped`). Calibration frames, masters, unreadable headers, and BITPIX 64 never enter. Import jobs live in memory (the latest 50 ended ones), survive the tab, and end with the process.
- **Evidence.** Roles and filters come from headers (`IMAGETYP`/`FRAME` through `readFitsProvenance`, `FILTER`), never from a file or directory name. An unclassified frame joins no group until an operator declares it a light; a light whose header names no filter, or a blank one, belongs to None (`NO_FILTER`) and is stacked like any other filter, unless an operator declares its filter (`operatorDeclared`, refused over a filter the header names, `409 verifiedMetadata`). Declarations are recorded among the warnings of the frame, and no header is ever rewritten. `OBJECT` is a hint: more than one target in a project is reported, never resolved; overlap is left to registration.
- **Filter groups.** A group is the lights of one filter: exactly the name their headers write, only trimmed (`filterKeyOf`), never classified, normalized, or merged, so names spelled differently (Ha, H-alpha, ha) are different filters; groups are computed, not stored, and sorted by filter. A group reports its frames, the usable ones (included with an available file), their exposure, and the number of distinct acquisition settings among them (`cohortKeyOf`: camera name, geometry, BITPIX, CFA, binning, gain, offset, readout mode, and exposure; unknown values never agree with known ones).
- **Files.** A frame is `available`, `missing`, or `changed` by the size and modification time of its file, checked when the service opens and on `POST /refresh` or `/projects/:projectId/refresh`; `corrupt` stays. An operator leaves a frame out or includes it again (`PATCH /frames/:sourceId`), and `DELETE /frames/:sourceId` takes it out of the project, never deleting its file.
- **Cohorts and calibration.** A cohort is the usable lights of one filter group that share their acquisition settings (`cohortIdOf`: group key and `cohortKeyOf`); cohorts are computed, and only the masters an operator chose are stored (`stack_cohorts`), so a choice returns with its frames. `GET /projects/:projectId/cohorts` matches each cohort against the Calibration Library (`resolvePreview` with its geometry and settings, its exposure, the median and the range of its sensor temperatures, and its most frequent filter name) and offers every matching dark, flat, and bias, newest first. `PATCH /projects/:projectId/cohorts/:cohortId` replaces the choice with `{ calibration: { dark?, flat?, bias? } }`, each `{ masterId, digest }` one of those offered (`409 incompatibleMaster` otherwise); a bias is dropped with a dark, which holds the pedestal, and a cohort with none chosen is not calibrated. Nothing asks for a camera, a reason, or a decision.
- **Analysis.** `POST /projects/:projectId/analyses` (202) refuses while another analysis or a run works (`409 jobConflict`), or while a chosen master is no longer available (`409 masterUnavailable`), and leases the chosen masters until it ends; a cohort with none chosen is measured uncalibrated. It measures every usable light whose measurement is missing or stale on a worker of its own (`imagestacking.worker.ts`, embedded by `app/build.ts`), one frame at a time: SHA-256, a digital read, `calibrate` with the masters of its cohort (`darkScaling: 'none'`, `flatRepresentation: 'calibratedNormalized'`, `invalidFlat: 'passthrough'`; a bias only without a dark), `detectStars` from SNR 5, and `measureSubframeQuality`. Each measurement is saved as it arrives with its fingerprint (measurement version, file size and time, chosen masters), so a cancelled or failed analysis keeps what it measured and the next one resumes, measuring again a frame whose measurement failed. Registration then runs on the same worker, since matching costs tens of milliseconds per frame (about a minute for a thousand frames), from the 100 brightest stars of each frame, which the server reads back: every frame onto the reference of its group (the pinned frame, or the best by `referenceQualityScore`) and every group reference onto the project reference (that of the pinned group, or the group reference with the most stars); a group that does not register fails alone. The transform of a frame onto the project grid is the composition of both, so a stack resamples it once. A worker silent for two minutes is terminated; `POST /analyses/:jobId/cancel` stops one between frames.
- **Validation.** `GET /projects/:projectId/validation` reads no pixel and lists findings: blockers (`noLights`, `flatWithoutPedestal` of a cohort with a flat but neither a dark nor a bias, whose pedestal divided by the flat would imprint its dust and vignetting inverted, `masterUnavailable`, `masterIncompatible`, `analysisStale` when a frame, a chosen master, a reference, or the frames of a group changed since, `registrationFailed` of a group), reviews (`multipleTargets`), and warnings (`partialCalibration` of a cohort without a flat, and of one without a dark or a bias when it has no flat either, `multipleSetups`, and frames that stay out of the stack: `measurementFailed`, `registrationFailed` of a frame, `belowThresholds` of the quality thresholds in `PUT /projects/:projectId/settings`), with an estimate of the memory of a stack (26 bytes per sample of the largest frame). Settings also hold the reference pins; a frame failing a threshold stays in the project.
- **Runs.** `POST /projects/:projectId/preflight` answers what a run would do with a recipe (`StackingRecipe`: combination, weighting, normalization, interpolation, minimum coverage, sigma limits) and a memory budget (2 GiB by default): per filter group, its frames, its exposure, a master it would reuse, and its plan (`integrationPlan`: in memory for sum and the averages, otherwise spilled to staging at 5 bytes per frame and pixel of every channel plus one, and combined in strips of rows that fit the budget), with what stops it: a blocker of validation (`409 notReady`), a group that does not fit the budget (`insufficientMemory`), a spill larger than the free space of the staging disk (`insufficientSpace`), or a chosen master no longer available (`masterUnavailable`). `POST /projects/:projectId/runs` (202) with a `token` starts one and answers its integration job; a run is a job of the process only, never stored (only the masters it publishes are), so the same token answers the same job while the process keeps it, and a failed run names in its job error each filter group that failed and why. No run or analysis starts while another works (`409 jobConflict`). A group integrates its usable lights whose measurement is current, that registered, and that pass the thresholds, the reference of the group first. On the worker each frame is read in digital numbers, calibrated as it was measured, debayered when it is a color mosaic, and resampled once onto the project grid (the size of the project reference) through the composition of its transform onto its group reference with that of the group onto the project; pixels of an unusable flat are masked (`invalidFlatMask`). It is matched to the warped group reference (`solveGlobalNormalizationPlanes`, per channel) on a nearest-neighbour resampling of both, which keeps their samples as they are: the interpolation of the recipe smooths noise by an amount that depends on the subpixel phase of each frame, which a dispersion estimator would read as a gain. The frame is then resampled with the interpolation of the recipe, weighted (`frameWeight`), and combined (`accumulateSamples` or `combineStrip`); a pixel covered by fewer frames than the minimum coverage is NaN. Masters are never cropped, so every master of a project shares its grid. A run is cancelled through `POST /runs/:runId/cancel`, and a worker silent for two minutes is terminated.
- **Products.** Each group is published as soon as it is staged, so a cancelled or failed run keeps what it published: its frames and chosen masters are compared again with what the run planned, its row is written as publishing (`stack_products`, with its frames in `stack_product_sources`), its directory renamed into `products/<productId>/` (`master.fit`, Float32 with a header written from metadata, and `coverage.fit`, the frames that covered each pixel), and only then published. A group whose inputs (`INTEGRATION_VERSION`, frames with their SHA-256, weights, and transforms, chosen masters, grid, and recipe) match a published master that is still ready reuses it. A product is `stale` while a frame it integrated left the project, is not available, or was measured with other content, or while a chosen master is no longer available, and `unavailable` while a file is missing or of another size; it is never rewritten. `GET /projects/:projectId/products` lists them, `GET /products/:productId` adds the paths of its files (to open them in Images) and its frames, and `DELETE /products/:productId` removes one, refused while a composite reads it (`409 productInUse`, also enforced by `stack_composite_sources`). On start, a product left publishing becomes published when its master has the recorded size and SHA-256 and is forgotten otherwise, one left deleting loses its directory and row, a directory of `products/` without a row moves to `orphaned/`, and staging is emptied.
- **Composition.** A composition (`StackingComposition`) mixes planes of masters of one project, never light frames, into linear RGB: each source is `{ productId, plane, gain, offset }`, each output channel the weighted mean of its sources (`mixChannelRows`), and an optional luminance source replaces the luminance of the mix by a weight, matched by background and scale first and keeping channel ratios (`blendLuminanceRows`). `POST /projects/:projectId/compositions/validate` reports blockers (a source that is not a master of the project or has no such plane, masters on different grids or unavailable, an empty channel, a weight, gain, or offset out of range, a luminance out of range) and warnings (a stale master), with the fingerprint of the inputs (`COMPOSITION_VERSION`, the composition, the SHA-256 of each master) and a published composite it would reuse. `POST /projects/:projectId/compositions/preview` answers a JPEG at most 768 pixels on its longest side of every k-th row and column, mixed by the same functions and stretched by one linked automatic curve for display; only the latest preview is computed, and an earlier one answers `409 superseded`. `POST /projects/:projectId/compositions` (202) composes on the worker, one composition at a time: a first pass over strips of rows finds the box of the pixels every mixed master covers and samples the luminance match, a second writes the cropped channels, joined into one Float32 `composite.fit` in `composites/<compositeId>/` (`stack_composites`, published like a product, with its origin on the project grid). Its masters cannot be deleted while it or a preview reads them. A composite is `stale` while a master it mixes is not ready; `GET /projects/:projectId/composites`, `GET /composites/:compositeId` (with its path), `DELETE /composites/:compositeId`, and `POST /compositions/:jobId/cancel` complete it.
- **Boundaries.** `GET /api/image-stacking` (`ImageStackingSnapshot`), `imageStacking:changed`, and `imageStacking:jobUpdated` carry an epoch per process and a revision per change, and no paths; the progress of a long step (rows of a strip, frames registered) is published at most four times a second, and its last step always; lists show file names, and a path comes only from `GET /frames/:sourceId` (to open it in Images) and from the candidates of an import, which the operator chose.
- **Tool.** Tools → Image Stacking (`web/src/tools/imagestacking/`, `/tools/image-stacking[/<project id>]`) keeps only Import Frames in its header and Check Files by the project, and its sections scroll on their own on a phone. It mirrors the server through `projects.ts` in `SERVER_STORES`, and fetches the project, its groups, and a page of frames again whenever a change arrives. Filters lists the groups; Frames filters by group, role, file state, inclusion, and name, and leaves a frame out, includes it again, or removes it from the project (after a confirmation, never deleting its file) from its row; the panel shows how each value of a frame is known, opens it in Images, and declares a role or a filter the header does not say. An import chooses directories, and files several at a time (the `multiple` file chooser), both choosers starting in the directory listed last (`lastStackingDirectory`, view state in localStorage), reads their headers, and adds nothing until its review is committed: every usable candidate starts checked, a check, Select All, and Deselect All hold across pages (all but the unchecked, or only the checked), and committing adds the checked ones and closes the dialog. Calibration shows one card per cohort with a list of the matching masters of each kind, named by id, date, and frames, saved as soon as one is chosen (the bias list is off while a dark is chosen), and a flat chosen without a dark or a bias is flagged as needing one; Quality starts and cancels the analysis, edits the project reference and the thresholds (numeric fields, 0 for none), lists the measurements and transforms of the frames of one group with their reference pin, fetched again as the running analysis measures each frame, and shows the reviews and notes of the validation with only its blockers that keep an analysis from starting (`noLights` of the project and `masterUnavailable`, `blocksAnalysis`), which alone disable Analyze; the blockers of a stack are shown by Integration. Integration edits the recipe and the memory budget, shows the preflight of each filter, starts a run with a new token, and follows and cancels it; Results lists the masters with their state, opens a master or its coverage in Images, and deletes one; Color resolves a preset (RGB, LRGB, SHO, HOO, LSHO, `presets.ts`) to the newest usable mono master of each filter or names what is missing, edits the gains, the weights of each channel, and the luminance (Custom), previews the composition as the server renders it, and composes and lists the composites. The project, its cohorts, its validation, its products, and its composites are fetched again on every change.
