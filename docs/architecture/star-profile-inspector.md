# Star Profile Inspector Contract

Part of the [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) of Nebulosa.

- `POST /api/images/:id/star-profile` (`createStarProfileRoutes`, `api/src/starprofile.ts`) measures the star a person pointed at in an image document, for as long as the request lasts. It is a short, explicit interaction, so unlike the analyses of `analysis.ts` it has no job, event, snapshot, or kept result, and holds no device. Its body is a `StarProfileRequest`: the point (0-based pixel, integer centres), the `model`, `searchRadius`, `maximumRadius`, `minSNR`, and `detected` when a star detection already found a star there.
- It answers 200 with a `StarProfileResult`: `measured`, even when the star is degraded or invalid, as its `flags`, `valid`, and `quality` say, or `noStar` with the reason `noPeak` or `ambiguous`. A closed image is 404, an aborted request 409 `superseded`, too many requests waiting 503 `queueFull`, and a failed measurement 500 `workerFailed` or `timeout`.
- Measurements run one at a time on a worker of their own (`api/src/starprofile.worker.ts`), at most 8 waiting, in arrival order. A request retains its image from the start. It reads only a region around the point (`ImageStore.detectionRegion`, see [Shared Image Contracts](shared-image.md)), reaching `2 × searchRadius + maximumRadius + 4` pixels on every side, so a click never copies the frame and a region edge never flags a star: `nearBorder` and `clipped` only come from the frame edges. An abort takes a request out of the queue; one on the worker cannot be interrupted, so it finishes, its result is dropped, and no other request of the same image is touched. A worker that dies or runs for 10 s is replaced.
- The toolkit does the science (`inspectStarRegion`, `api/src/starprofile.analysis.ts`): `detectStars` finds the star nearest the point within `searchRadius` on a region wide enough for it to start that star (`regionHalfSize`), and `measureStarProfile` measures it, sizing its aperture up to `maximumRadius`. The brightest sample of the region is never taken as the star. Two stars less than 1 px from being equally near are `ambiguous`, and so is a centroid that moved more than `searchRadius` from its candidate, which followed something else. `detectStars` cannot look within `starDetectionBorder(maximumRadius)` of a frame edge (34 px for the default aperture radius of 32 px), so with no candidate there the point itself is measured, and kept only with an HFD of at least 1 px and a peak of at least 6 deviations; the SNR of the profile proves nothing here, since it counts positive flux only and grows with the aperture on a bare sky. Elsewhere no candidate is `noPeak`, as for a faint star whose search box holds a much brighter neighbor, which the detector leaves out as ASTAP does.
- Images → Star Profile Inspector (`web/src/images/StarProfileInspectorPanel.tsx`, store `web/src/stars/starprofile.ts`) is an image tool of every document. Selecting it runs nothing, and neither does editing its settings: only a click on the image (`onImageClick`, after Star Detection in the click owners) or Analyze Star on the star selected. A click on a star of a loaded star detection sends that star as `detected`; a click off the image is ignored. Hiding the overlays stops the clicks without touching the star, and on a camera tab a click holds the frame (`holdCameraFrame`).
- The latest measurement lives in `ImageViewState.starProfile`, tab memory only, bound to the image id it measured: `starProfileOf` never returns it for another image. A newer measurement aborts the one before it, an answer commits only under its own token, and `clearStarProfile` aborts and drops it when the document closes or the transaction of a camera tab shows another frame. Requests of other tabs are their own. The marker (`web/src/stars/StarProfileOverlay.tsx`) is drawn while the tool is open, in image pixels. The preview (`web/src/stars/StarPreview.tsx`) draws the samples of the response from the sky to the peak, never the rendering of the document. The plot (`web/src/stars/StarProfilePlot.tsx`) and the texts of a profile are shared with the Guiding panel. Widths show arcseconds only under a solution of the same image whose scale is the same along both axes (`isotropicPixelScaleAt`).
- The settings (model, search radius, aperture radius, minimum SNR, and what the preview draws) are one profile preference, `ProfileSettings.starProfile` (`starProfileSettings`, `setStarProfileSettings`), shared by every document. A measured star is never stored.
- `requestedModel` is what was asked and `profile.model` what was delivered: a rejected Moffat fit falls back to the moments, with `moffat.reason` and the `poorFit` flag. FWHM is Gaussian-equivalent for the moments and fitted for Moffat; neither is the half-maximum width of a cut. The HFD is the curve-of-growth one of the toolkit under either model, and it is not comparable with a `DetectedStar` HFD.
- Positions are pixels of the image, centroids included. The cuts go through the pixel nearest the centroid, with `offsets` fractional from the centroid and `values` above the background of the profile; `model` is the accepted fit at those same pixels (`moffatProfileValue`) and is absent without one. Cuts and preview reach `2 × HFD` pixels, from 12 (cuts) or 15 (preview) up to 32. Intensities are normalized samples, and `conversion` (`SampleConversion`) turns them into the scale of the source.
- Limitations: a star whose aperture holds a much brighter neighbor may answer `ambiguous`, since the moments follow the neighbor. A Moffat fit is often rejected on a faint star, whose residual is mostly noise, or a nearly Gaussian one, and the moments stand for it. Saturation is judged against the full scale: it is unknown on an image the read rescaled (`saturationKnown` false), and a debayered mosaic averages it away at the edge of a star. The SNR is the background-limited estimate of the toolkit, not a photon-noise model. A mosaic without a trusted pattern is measured as is.
