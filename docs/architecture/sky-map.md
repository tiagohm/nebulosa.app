# Sky Map Contract

Part of the [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) of Nebulosa.

- One tick drives the star map and the selection (`#followClock` of `web/src/sky/planetarium.ts`): every `SKY_REALTIME_TICK_MS` (15 s) while the clock follows the real time, frequently while playing, and once for a fixed instant.
  Each tick reads the instant once, shows it on the map, and refreshes the selection's position and events for that same instant (`refreshSkySelection` with the tick's time): accurately on every real-time tick, in the fast mode every few ticks while playing, and accurately once a fixed instant settles.
  The time bar's own redraws are display only.
  The map catalog (`GET /api/sky/catalog/stars`, `/api/sky/catalog/dsos`, and `/api/sky/constellations`) is J2000, the stars with their proper motion in mas per Julian year, and independent of any instant, so it is downloaded once per tab (`loadSkyMapCatalog` of `web/src/sky/sky.ts`).
  The browser moves each star linearly by its proper motion from J2000 and turns every position by the toolkit's IAU 2006/2000A precession-nutation matrix of the instant (`skyMapAt` of `web/src/sky/mapdata.ts`), the conversion the server made before; an instant more than `MAP_DATA_EPOCH_MS` (a Julian year) from the map data's, reached at any tick, playing or not, computes it again in place, with no request (`mapDataCovers`).
- The map computes its own sidereal time in the browser (`web/src/sky/celestial/time.ts`): UT1 is UTC within the IERS predictions of the server (a year beyond now, within 0.9 s), and after them it falls behind UTC by the growth of the toolkit's best-estimate ΔT since their end (`ut1MinusUtc`), as the server's Earth orientation extrapolates it, so the horizon drawn keeps the altitudes the server computes decades ahead.
  Before 1960 a reading is UT on both sides, the server adding ΔT to reach TT (see [Tools Integration](tools-integration.md)).
- The map's density follows the size of its disk as well as the zoom (`densityZoom` of `web/src/sky/celestial/visibility.ts`): deep-sky symbols and the names of stars, deep-sky objects, and constellations read the zoom times the share of a 360 px disk radius the map has (at least 0.35, at most 1), so a phone draws the whole sky with the few names a desktop shows zoomed out, and the names return as it zooms in; picking reads the same limits, so only drawn objects and names are picked.
  No name is drawn over another (`SkyLabelSpace` of `web/src/sky/celestial/labels.ts`), in a fixed priority: the names of the Sun, the Moon, the planets, asteroids, and comets are always drawn (one that would overlap an earlier one moves to the other side of its body, or just below or above it); then star names; then deep-sky names; then satellite names, the selected satellite's always; then constellation names. A lower name that would overlap a higher one, or one of its own kind drawn before, is left out; the hover and selection overlay still names a star or a deep-sky object, and a constellation whose name is left out can still be picked at its anchor.
  The layers draw on three canvases that redraw independently: moving-body and star names are placed once per frame (`RenderState.frame`) for every layer to read, deep-sky names are kept as their layer last drew them, and satellite and constellation names, drawn last, are placed against both. The renderer redraws the moving-body, star, deep-sky, and constellation-name layers together (`linkLayers`), so no canvas keeps names placed against another's previous frame, while a satellite update (every 250 ms while tracking) redraws only its own canvas (`markMovingBodyDirty(undefined, true)`).
- The Display panel groups the layers into Objects, Names, and Lines, each section summarizing how many of its layers are shown.
- The hover (and the tooltip of the hovered object) clears when the pointer leaves the map, unless a gesture is in progress.
- Picking tolerates fingers: a touch or pen tap picks within a larger radius and stays a tap over a longer travel than a mouse click (`touchPickRadius` and `touchTapSlop` of `InteractionOptions` of `web/src/sky/celestial/`).
- On the local sky zoomed in to `SKY_ANCHOR_ZOOM` or more, a tick that changes the instant keeps the view on the same point of the sky (recentered after the update) instead of the same point of the horizon, unless the mount is followed;
  at lower zoom the horizon stays still.
- Selected objects the map data lacks are remembered for the tab (`RememberedObjects` of `web/src/sky/remembered.ts`, at most `REMEMBERED_LIMIT`, the least recently selected forgotten first, in memory only, kept when the map is replaced by the other kind of chart): minor bodies from SBDB as moving bodies drawn at every zoom whatever their magnitude (`alwaysVisible`), and catalog stars and deep-sky objects fainter than the map data as selectable shapes with their name.
  They stay drawn and can be selected again on the map.
  The selected one follows the Inspector's position of each tick; the other minor bodies are moved with local (`fast`) positions at every map update.
  Satellites are never remembered: the satellite layer draws them (see [Artificial Satellites Contract](artificial-satellites.md)).
- Sky point: a click (or tap) on an empty part of the map, where nothing is picked, selects the point clicked, so the mount can be sent between objects; a click on an object selects the object, and a click below the local horizon or that ends a pan does nothing.
  The point is converted from the frame of the loaded catalogs to J2000 with the transpose of their precession-nutation matrix (`fromDate` of `ofDateMatrix`, `Planetarium.#selectSkyPoint`) and selected as the `SkyObjectRef` `{ kind: 'coordinates', rightAscension, declination }` (J2000 radians, key `coordinates:<ra>:<dec>`) named Sky Point.
  There is at most one: it is drawn as a small cross (`skyPoint` shape) only while it is the selection, marked again in the frame of new map data, and replaced by the next empty click.
  `/api/sky/position` and the locator of the mounts resolve it as a catalog object without proper motion, so the Object Inspector shows its place, constellation, and rise, transit, and set like any fixed object, and Slew to Point sends the mount through the same slew route, resolved at the real current time and refused below the horizon.
  It is never a favorite and is added to the Observing Planner as fixed coordinates under its name.
- Asteroids and comets of the SPICE Kernel Library (`kernelBodies` of `web/src/sky/kernels.sky.ts`, from the library mirror of the tab, `spiceKernelsState`): every small body whose kernel Horizons generated (source kind `horizons`) and that the ephemeris may use (not disabled, invalid, or missing), once per SPK-ID, is drawn like a remembered minor body (`Planetarium.setKernelBodies`): at every zoom, under the designation of its kernel without the SPK-ID, a comet told by its SPK-ID (1000001 to 1999999) until SBDB data says otherwise, moved with local (`fast`) positions at every map update, and selectable.
  A body that leaves the library stops being drawn unless the map remembers it, as it does once selected.
  Each new kernel generation of the library (a kernel added, removed, turned on or off, or the offline switch) recomputes the selection (`refreshSkySelection`, whatever the age of its position and the clock mode) and every body for the instant shown, so the source that takes over is shown at once rather than at the next tick, or never for a fixed instant.
  Without its kernel, a minor body follows the chain of its mode: the selection (accurate) is given by Horizons, then by the two-body orbit of its SBDB elements, and the other bodies of the map (fast) by that orbit first, which drifts from the integrated orbit away from the epoch of the elements (over a degree for 161P/Hartley-IRAS in 2026 from elements of 2021).
- The projection of the map is fixed: the local sky is centred on the observer's zenith and the equatorial chart on its own hemisphere, and no feature ever moves the projection centre or reprojects the map around a coordinate.
  "Centre on" a coordinate (the mount, a coordinate or footprint sent from an image) only pans the screen view with `Planetarium.centerOn`/`Celestial.centerOnCoordinate`, keeping the zoom and orientation;
  a point the map cannot project (below the local horizon, or outside the hemisphere the equatorial chart projects) leaves the view unchanged and is said to be so.
  Likewise, a selection whose refracted altitude at the instant shown is negative is said on the local sky to be below the horizon, so a choice the map cannot draw is never silent.
- Handoffs from a solved image (`web/src/sky/footprint.ts`, `skyImageState`, tab memory): Open Coordinates in Sky (the image context menu, and the details of a marker) marks a position and pans to it;
  Show Footprint in Sky (Images → Plate Solver) draws the outline the selected solution gives to the image borders (sampled along each side, broken where the WCS cannot unproject) as a dashed shape of its own beside the framing, with a cross at its centre, and pans to that centre.
  Both keep their positions in the J2000 (ICRS or FK5 J2000) frame of the solution and are converted for the map with the toolkit's IAU 2006/2000A precession-nutation matrix at the instant the catalogs were computed for (`ofDateMatrix`, the conversion of `web/src/sky/mapdata.ts`), so they line up with the stars drawn;
  the matrix is computed once per map data load, never per tick, and nothing is requested.
  A footprint is drawn only while its document still shows that image and is dropped otherwise (the document closed, or its camera tab shows another frame);
  the mark stays until replaced or cleared from the Sky toolbar, where a toggle also hides the footprint and shows it again in place, without moving the view (sending a footprint shows it).
  Neither changes the Sky selection, the clock, or the mount.
- Meteor shower radiants (`web/src/sky/meteors.sky.ts`, `MeteorRadiantLayer`, the `meteorRadiants` layer of the display, shown by default): the radiants of the showers active at the instant shown (`POST /api/meteor-showers/radiants`) and the selected shower's (`/states`, drawn even outside its activity and with the layer hidden), as selectable custom shapes (`meteorRadiant`) with their code.
  The server gives them geocentric J2000 and the map converts them with the precession-nutation matrix of its data (`ofDateMatrix`), as the stars, so they are precessed once.
  They are asked for again only when the instant shown moves more than an hour from the last request (about 0.05° of drift), the catalog revision or the layer changes, or another shower is selected, and a failure waits 10 s; nothing is fetched per tick or frame.
  A radiant is a direction, not an object (`SkyObjectRef` `meteorShower`): `/api/sky/position` refuses it (`422 unsupportedTarget`), the locator of the mounts never resolves it, the selection loads no position, and the Meteor Shower Inspector (`web/src/sky/meteor/`) reads its state at the Sky's instant every minute: activity and its source, the radiant J2000 and of date, and for the site its geometric altitude and azimuth, the Sun, and the Moon, with its next maximum.
  It offers Center on Radiant (a pan through `showCoordinateInSky`, without a mark) and Open in Meteor Showers, never Slew, framing, or a planner target.
  The selected shower's drift path over its nearest occurrence (`/path`) is drawn dashed in the same frame as the radiants, never joined across a gap, asked for once per shower and catalog and again only when the instant shown leaves the occurrence by more than a month; the radiant itself stays at the Sky's instant, and the Inspector names the dates the path covers.
  The search lists matching showers (at most 5, never removed records, the curated ones first) in a Meteor Showers section.
- Handoff to the Framing tool: Open in Framing (the Object Inspector of an object, the Sun, the Moon, the Sky point, and the mount marker) centers the preview of the Framing tool on the J2000 position of the selection and opens the tool on the Equipment Setup's framing (`skySelectionCenter` and `openFramingAt` of `web/src/tools/framing/framing.ts`), under the selection's name: a catalog object's catalog position, the astrometric J2000 position of anything else once computed (disabled until then), and the mount's position of date converted to J2000 (`mountCenter`) at the click, offered only while its position is shown.
  It changes nothing of the Sky; the Sky's own Framing panel, opened from its toolbar, still draws the framings around the selection.
- Handoff to the Orbital Mechanics Explorer: Explore Orbit (`ObjectActions.tsx`, for a minor body) opens the tool on the body by its SPK-ID and name only (`exploreMinorBody`), never by a position, and changes nothing of the Sky (see [Orbital Mechanics Explorer Contract](orbital-mechanics-explorer.md)).
- Handoffs with the Observing Planner: Add to Observing Planner (`ObjectActions.tsx`, for a catalog object, a major body other than the Sun, a minor body by its SPK-ID, or the Sky point as fixed coordinates, `plannerTarget`) opens the tool with the target pending (`addToObservingPlanner`), where a dialog adds it to a plan chosen by the operator;
  Show in Sky of the planner (`showTargetInSky`) selects a catalog object, a body, or a minor body (located by the Sky's own ephemeris, never the planner's trajectory) through `selectSkyObject` without moving the view, and marks fixed coordinates through `skyImageState`, panning to them.
  Neither moves the Sky clock (positions are at the Sky's own time), the mount, the profile, or its site.
- Favorites (`web/src/sky/favorites.ts`): a star (`FavoriteButton`) on every object the search lists and in the header of the Object Inspector, whatever its view (the Sun, the Moon, a satellite, a meteor shower), marks or unmarks it; the mount marker and the Sky point have none, and a catalog object is marked once its catalog data arrived, since its type is not known before.
  Satellites are favorites by NORAD id in the satellite favorites of Artificial Satellites (`satelliteFavorites`), so marking one in the Sky also draws it on the map and lists it in that tool; every other object is kept as the search listed it (`skyFavorites`, see [Persistence and State](persistence-and-state.md)).
  The search lists the favorites instead of searching while its Favorites toggle is on, the text then only filtering them, and whenever its field is empty (no list without favorites, unless the toggle is on), by section and name: the kept ones filtered in the tab (name, designations, and type, ignoring case, accents, and spaces), and the favorite satellites asked from the satellite catalog by id and text (`GET /api/satellites?ids&q`, at most 100, saying how many more).
  The toggle is tab memory and resets with the page.
- The search reads Bayer and Flamsteed designations by the IAU abbreviation or the genitive of the constellation and the Greek letter by name, symbol, or abbreviation, with SIMBAD's own (`alf`, `tet`, `ksi`) too, so "Alpha Centauri", "alf Ori", and "58 Orionis" all resolve.
  Catalog stars stop at magnitude 8 (`bin/src/sky.catalog.ts`), which the empty result says.
- `POST /api/sky/position` returns the rise, transit, and set for an observer in both modes (they are computed locally and cached per hour), so the Visibility of the Object Inspector always shows the instant of its Position.
  Rise and set are the instants the airless altitude crosses the standard horizon: −34′ for a point, −50′ for the Sun, and −49.5′ for the Moon, its mean semidiameter below the horizon of a point, on its topocentric place.
  Transit is the crossing of the meridian, the hour angle going from negative to positive, not the highest point, with its altitude read from the same model.
  Horizontal altitudes hold the refraction of a standard atmosphere (ERFA from 5°, Sæmundsson below, fading out under the horizon).
- Every request of the process to JPL (Horizons, SBDB, CAD) goes through one `SsdRequestQueue` (`api/src/ssd.ts`), since its fair-use policy allows one request at a time: tasks run in order, the budget of each starts with it, the wait for a turn is limited to `SSD_QUEUE_WAIT_MS` (15 s), identical requests are shared, and the last caller to leave stops one.
  Requests name the client in a `User-Agent` with the version `app/src/main.ts` gives to `identifyJplClient` and the repository as contact.
  A 429 or 503 is never retried: it pauses the queue for its `Retry-After`, 30 s by default and 5 minutes at most, and the queue refuses tasks meanwhile; only SBDB and CAD answers can do so, since the Horizons adapter does not expose the status.
  An accurate position may therefore wait behind another JPL request, and a refusal of the queue is a Horizons `timeout` attempt of the chain without counting against the Horizons circuit breaker.
- Positions of the Sun, the Moon, the planets, Pluto, and minor bodies (`SkyEphemeris` of `api/src/sky/ephemeris.ts`, given the `spiceKernels` runtime) follow the [Ephemeris Chain](ephemeris-chain.md) in the mode of the request: accurate by default (kernels, Horizons, theories) and fast for the map (kernels, theories, Horizons), with `source` (`spk`, `horizons`, `analytical`, or `catalog` for a catalog object and `sgp4` for a satellite), `mode`, `attempts`, and for `spk` the `kernels` (the generation and the ids of the kernels used). The kernels give a position when the kernel set resolves the target by its NAIF code (the body code, never a system barycenter for its planet, or the SBDB SPK-ID), the Earth (399), and the Sun (10) at every epoch the calculation needs, light-time emission epochs included;
  a single miss abandons the whole kernel calculation, which runs whole on the next source.
  Offline, Horizons is never asked and a minor body is resolved from the SBDB answers already cached, never asking SBDB; a minor body with neither kernels nor cached elements answers `503 offline`.
  No source answers `503` with the code and attempts of the chain; a semantic Horizons failure answers `502 rejected`.
  Events follow the fast chain without Horizons: the kernels when they cover the whole ±12 h window, else the theories, and are cached per kernel generation.
  Every computation of positions consumes them through the [Ephemeris Chain](ephemeris-chain.md); the Sun and Moon Inspectors, the converter, and the satellites keep their own models.
